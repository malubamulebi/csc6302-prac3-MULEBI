# Task 5: Ethical Reflection on Face Detection

**CSC6302 — Computer Vision | Practical 3**

Face detection technologies can support security and monitoring systems, but their deployment introduces ethical concerns relating to privacy, consent and algorithmic fairness. In Zambia, one potential application is CCTV surveillance in public spaces around Lusaka. Although such systems may improve security, they could also enable excessive monitoring of individuals without their knowledge or meaningful consent.

A significant risk is the possibility of incorrect face detections being used to make decisions about individuals. During this practical, the Haar cascade detector produced several false positives and failed to correctly identify faces in the group photograph. If deployed in a surveillance environment, similar errors could result in innocent individuals being incorrectly flagged for further investigation. Furthermore, differences in model performance across skin tones, lighting conditions and facial appearances could lead to unfair outcomes for particular demographic groups. The results of this practical demonstrate detection errors, although they do not independently establish demographic bias.

An important safeguard would be to require human verification before any detection is used to justify action against an individual. Additional measures should include clear public notices, restrictions on image storage, controlled access to recorded data and fairness testing using representative Zambian datasets.

For this practical, I personally contacted the other three individuals appearing in the group photograph and obtained their consent to use the image for the assignment. They agreed and supported its use. This demonstrates the importance of respecting individuals' choices when collecting and processing identifiable images.

Overall, face detection systems should be deployed with transparency, accountability and appropriate privacy safeguards to ensure that technological benefits do not compromise individual rights.
